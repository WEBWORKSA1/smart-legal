    Legal dictionary

    124+ legal terms in plain English

    Every definition is written to be understood on the first read. Search instantly or jump by letter.

    ABCDEFGHIJLMNOPQRSTUVWZ

    A

    Acquittal
    A finding by a judge or jury that a defendant is not guilty of a criminal charge.
    Adjournment
    Postponing a court hearing to a later date.
    Affidavit
    A written statement of facts sworn or affirmed to be true before an authorised official, used as evidence.
    Affirmative defence
    A defence where the defendant admits the basic facts but raises new facts that excuse liability, such as self-defence.
    Alimony
    Payments from one spouse to the other after separation or divorce; also called spousal support or maintenance.
    Alternative dispute resolution (ADR)
    Ways of settling disputes outside a courtroom, such as mediation and arbitration.
    Amicus curiae
    A 'friend of the court' — a person or group that is not a party but offers information or arguments in a case.
    Answer
    The defendant's formal written response to a complaint in a civil lawsuit.
    Appeal
    Asking a higher court to review and change the decision of a lower court.
    Appellant
    The party who brings an appeal.
    Arbitration
    A private process where a neutral arbitrator hears both sides and makes a decision that is usually binding.
    Arraignment
    The court hearing where a defendant is formally told the charges and asked to enter a plea.
    Assault
    Causing someone to fear immediate harmful contact, or in many places, actually applying force without consent.
    Attorney-client privilege
    The rule that confidential communications between a lawyer and client for legal advice are protected from disclosure.

    B

    Bail
    Money or conditions set by a court to allow a person accused of a crime to be released while the case continues.
    Bankruptcy
    A legal process for people or businesses who cannot pay their debts, which may discharge or reorganise those debts.
    Bench trial
    A trial decided by a judge rather than a jury.
    Beneficiary
    A person or organisation entitled to receive benefits from a will, trust, insurance policy or account.
    Beyond a reasonable doubt
    The high standard of proof required for a criminal conviction.
    Breach of contract
    Failing to do what a contract requires without a lawful excuse.
    Brief
    A written legal argument submitted to a court.
    Burden of proof
    The obligation to prove the facts in dispute, and how convincingly they must be proved.

    C

    Cause of action
    The legal basis — a set of facts recognised by law — that entitles someone to sue.
    Cease and desist
    A letter or order demanding that someone stop an activity, such as infringement or harassment.
    Chargeback
    A card payment reversal requested by a cardholder through their bank, often for fraud or non-delivery.
    Civil law
    Law dealing with disputes between private parties, as opposed to criminal prosecution; also a legal system based on codes.
    Class action
    A lawsuit brought by one or a few people on behalf of a larger group with similar claims.
    Common law
    Law developed by judges through decisions in individual cases, used in the US, Canada (outside Quebec), the UK, India and Australia.
    Comparative negligence
    A rule that reduces a person's compensation by their own percentage of fault.
    Compensatory damages
    Money awarded to make up for actual losses such as medical bills, lost income and pain.
    Complaint
    The document that starts a civil lawsuit and sets out the claims against the defendant.
    Consideration
    Something of value exchanged between parties that helps make a contract binding.
    Contempt of court
    Disobeying a court order or disrupting court proceedings, which can lead to fines or jail.
    Contingency fee
    A lawyer's fee that is a percentage of the money recovered, payable only if the case succeeds.
    Conveyancing
    The legal work of transferring ownership of real property.
    Copyright
    Legal protection for original creative works such as text, music, art and software.
    Counterclaim
    A claim made by a defendant against the plaintiff in the same lawsuit.
    Covenant
    A formal promise in a contract or deed, such as a restriction on land use.
    Cross-examination
    Questioning of a witness by the party who did not call that witness.
    Custody
    Legal responsibility for a child's care and major decisions; often split into legal and physical custody.

    D

    Damages
    Money a court orders one party to pay another for loss or injury.
    Declaratory judgment
    A court ruling that clarifies the parties' rights without ordering damages or action.
    Deed
    A signed legal document that transfers or confirms ownership of property.
    Default judgment
    A decision against a party who failed to respond or appear in a case.
    Defamation
    A false statement of fact that harms someone's reputation; libel if written, slander if spoken.
    Defendant
    The person or organisation being sued or accused of a crime.
    Deposition
    Sworn out-of-court testimony recorded for use in a lawsuit.
    Discovery
    The pre-trial exchange of evidence and information between parties.
    Dismissal
    Ending a case without a full trial, either with prejudice (cannot refile) or without prejudice (can refile).
    Due diligence
    Careful investigation before a transaction or decision.
    Due process
    The right to fair procedures before the government takes away life, liberty or property.

    E

    Easement
    A right to use someone else's land for a specific purpose, such as a driveway or utility line.
    Estate
    Everything a person owns at death, including property, money and debts.
    Eviction
    The legal process by which a landlord removes a tenant from a rental property.
    Evidence
    Testimony, documents, objects and other material used to prove facts in a case.
    Executor
    The person named in a will to manage the estate and carry out the will's instructions.
    Expungement
    A court process that seals or erases a criminal record in certain circumstances.

    F

    Fair use
    A doctrine allowing limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes like commentary or education; similar to 'fair dealing'.
    Felony
    A serious crime, usually punishable by more than one year in prison.
    Fiduciary
    A person legally obliged to act in another's best interest, such as a trustee or executor.

    G

    Garnishment
    A court-ordered process taking money from wages or a bank account to pay a debt.
    Good faith
    Honest intention to deal fairly with others.
    Grand jury
    A group of citizens that decides whether there is enough evidence to charge someone with a serious crime.
    Guardian
    A person appointed to care for a minor or an adult who cannot care for themselves.

    H

    Habeas corpus
    A court order requiring authorities to justify why a person is being detained.
    Hearsay
    An out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of what it says; often not admissible, with many exceptions.

    I

    Indemnity
    A promise to cover another party's losses or legal costs.
    Indictment
    A formal written accusation of a serious crime.
    Injunction
    A court order requiring someone to do or stop doing something.
    Intestate
    Dying without a valid will.

    J

    Joint and several liability
    When several parties are responsible for a debt or harm, each can be required to pay the full amount.
    Judgment
    The final decision of a court in a case.
    Jurisdiction
    A court's authority to hear a case, or the geographic area where a set of laws applies.

    L

    Lien
    A legal claim against property to secure payment of a debt.
    Limitation period
    The deadline for starting a lawsuit; also called a statute of limitations.
    Litigation
    The process of resolving disputes through the courts.

    M

    Mediation
    A voluntary process where a neutral mediator helps parties reach their own agreement.
    Misdemeanour
    A less serious crime, usually punishable by a fine or less than a year in jail.
    Mitigation
    The duty to take reasonable steps to limit your losses after a breach or injury.
    Motion
    A formal request asking the court to make a ruling or order.

    N

    Negligence
    Failing to take reasonable care, causing harm to someone owed a duty of care.
    Non-compete clause
    A contract term restricting someone from working for competitors for a period after leaving.
    Non-disclosure agreement (NDA)
    A contract in which parties agree to keep certain information confidential.
    Notary public
    An official authorised to witness signatures and certify documents.

    O

    Objection
    A formal protest raised in court against evidence or a question.
    Ombudsman
    An independent official who investigates complaints against organisations or government.

    P

    Parole
    Supervised early release from prison.
    Plaintiff
    The person or organisation who starts a civil lawsuit; also called the claimant.
    Plea bargain
    An agreement where a defendant pleads guilty in exchange for a lesser charge or sentence.
    Pleadings
    The formal written statements of each party's claims and defences.
    Power of attorney
    A document authorising someone to act for another person in financial or health matters.
    Precedent
    A previous court decision that guides how later similar cases are decided.
    Preponderance of the evidence
    The usual civil standard of proof — more likely than not.
    Pro bono
    Legal work done free of charge for the public good.
    Probate
    The court process of validating a will and administering an estate.
    Probation
    A sentence allowing an offender to remain in the community under supervision and conditions.
    Punitive damages
    Extra damages meant to punish especially harmful conduct and deter it.

    Q

    Quitclaim deed
    A deed that transfers whatever interest the grantor has in property, without guarantees.

    R

    Restraining order
    A court order prohibiting someone from contacting or approaching another person.
    Retainer
    An advance payment to secure a lawyer's services, or the agreement to hire a lawyer.

    S

    Settlement
    An agreement resolving a dispute without a court decision.
    Small claims court
    A simplified court for lower-value disputes, usually without lawyers.
    Statute
    A written law passed by a legislature.
    Subpoena
    A court order requiring a person to testify or produce documents.
    Summary judgment
    A decision without a full trial because the key facts are not genuinely in dispute.
    Summons
    A document notifying a defendant that they are being sued and must respond.

    T

    Tenancy
    The right to occupy property under a lease or rental agreement.
    Testimony
    Evidence given by a witness under oath.
    Tort
    A civil wrong, other than breach of contract, that causes harm, such as negligence or defamation.
    Trademark
    A word, logo or sign that identifies the source of goods or services.
    Trust
    An arrangement where a trustee holds property for the benefit of beneficiaries.
    Trustee
    The person or institution that manages trust property for beneficiaries.

    U

    Undue influence
    Improper pressure that overpowers a person's free will, which can invalidate a will or contract.

    V

    Venue
    The specific court location where a case is heard.
    Verdict
    The decision of a jury or judge on the facts of a case.
    Void
    Having no legal effect from the start.
    Voidable
    Valid unless and until one party chooses to cancel it.

    W

    Waiver
    Voluntarily giving up a known right.
    Warrant
    A court order authorising police to make an arrest or carry out a search.
    Will
    A legal document stating how a person's property should be distributed after death.
    Witness
    A person who gives evidence about what they saw, heard or know.
    Workers' compensation
    Insurance benefits for employees injured on the job, usually without needing to prove fault.
    Writ
    A formal written court order.

    Z

    Zoning
    Local rules controlling how land and buildings in certain areas may be used.
