    Privacy, Data & Online Harms

    Your rights over personal data, data breaches, online harassment, defamation and AI — how to take control.

    Personal data now drives most of the economy, and privacy laws such as the GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, PIPEDA and India's DPDP Act give individuals real tools: the right to know what is collected, to get a copy, to correct it and often to delete it. Online harms like harassment and defamation also have legal remedies, though they move slower than the internet does.

    Your key rights

    • To know what personal data an organisation holds about you and why (right of access).
    • To correct inaccurate data and, in many cases, to request deletion.
    • To opt out of the sale or sharing of your data for advertising in several jurisdictions.
    • To be notified of certain data breaches that put you at risk.
    • To seek removal of defamatory, intimate or harassing content and, in some cases, damages.

    Step-by-step plan

    1. Send a data-access request. Use our GDPR/CCPA request generator; organisations usually must reply within about 30–45 days.
    2. Lock down accounts after a breach. Change passwords, enable two-factor authentication, freeze credit if financial data leaked.
    3. Preserve evidence of online harms. Screenshots with URLs and dates before content is deleted.
    4. Report to the platform. Use official reporting tools; note the report reference.
    5. Complain to the regulator. Data-protection authorities handle complaints for free.

    Documents to gather

    • Screenshots and URLs
    • Breach notification letters
    • Correspondence with the company
    • Identity-theft reports

    What it may cost

    Data requests and regulator complaints are free. Defamation and privacy lawsuits can be expensive; insurance or contingency arrangements sometimes apply.

    When to talk to a lawyer

    For serious defamation, intimate-image abuse, identity theft with large losses, stalking, or a company ignoring statutory requests.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I make a company delete my data?

    Often yes under GDPR-style laws, with exceptions such as legal record-keeping obligations.

    Is a bad online review defamation?

    Honest opinion and true statements are generally protected. False statements of fact that harm reputation may be defamatory.

    What should I do after a data breach?

    Change passwords, enable 2FA, monitor accounts and consider a credit freeze.

    Do privacy laws apply to small websites?

    Some laws have thresholds; others (like GDPR) apply broadly to anyone handling EU residents' data.

    This guide is general legal information, not legal advice. Laws differ by country, state and province and change over time. For advice on your situation, consult a licensed lawyer.
