    Immigration, Visas & Citizenship

    Work and study visas, family sponsorship, permanent residence, citizenship and removal — avoid the costly mistakes.

    Immigration law is federal or national in most countries and is famously technical: a missed form, wrong fee or late filing can cost months or a whole application. The safest approach is to treat official government sources as the final word and to be extremely wary of anyone promising guaranteed results.

    Your key rights

    • Everyone has the right to accurate information from official government immigration sources.
    • In many countries, people in removal or deportation proceedings may be represented by a lawyer (often at their own cost).
    • People fleeing persecution can generally apply for asylum or refugee protection.
    • You have the right to keep copies of everything you submit and to request your own immigration file in many systems.
    • Only licensed lawyers or officially authorised representatives may give immigration legal advice for a fee in most countries.

    Step-by-step plan

    1. Identify the exact category. Work, study, family, investor, humanitarian — each has different eligibility, forms and fees.
    2. Use official forms and fee schedules only. Download from the government website; fees and editions change regularly.
    3. Keep your status valid. Track expiry dates for visas, permits and passports. Apply for extensions before they lapse.
    4. Prepare translations and certified copies. Missing translations are a common cause of delays or refusals.
    5. Keep a complete copy of every application. Including the receipt notices and biometrics confirmations.
    6. Never miss a hearing or interview. Missing a removal hearing can lead to an order made in your absence.

    Documents to gather

    • Valid passport
    • Birth and marriage certificates (with translations)
    • Prior visas, permits and travel history
    • Proof of funds / employment
    • Police certificates
    • Medical exam results where required

    What it may cost

    Government fees vary by application. Lawyer fees are commonly flat-fee per application. Beware 'notarios' or consultants who are not authorised to practise immigration law.

    When to talk to a lawyer

    Always get professional advice if you have any past refusal, overstay, criminal record, removal proceedings, or if you are applying for asylum.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I work while my application is pending?

    Only if your current status or a specific work authorisation allows it. Working without authorisation can make you ineligible later.

    How long does processing take?

    Processing times are published by most immigration agencies and change monthly; check the official tracker for your category.

    What is a notario fraud?

    People who falsely claim to be qualified to give immigration advice. Verify any adviser with the bar or the official regulator.

    Can I travel during processing?

    Sometimes travel can abandon an application. Check whether you need advance travel permission before leaving.
