    Traffic Tickets & DUI

    Speeding tickets, licence points, impaired-driving charges and licence suspensions — your options before you pay.

    Paying a ticket is usually treated as pleading guilty. That can add points to your licence, raise insurance premiums for years and, for commercial drivers, threaten your job. Impaired-driving (DUI/DWI) charges are criminal in most places and carry far heavier consequences.

    Your key rights

    • You can usually contest a traffic ticket and request the evidence (radar calibration, officer notes).
    • You may be able to reduce points through traffic school or a plea to a lesser offence, depending on location.
    • For impaired-driving stops, you have rights to counsel and specific procedures must be followed for breath or blood tests.
    • Administrative licence suspensions often have their own short deadline to request a hearing.

    Step-by-step plan

    1. Read the ticket carefully. Note the offence, court, deadline and whether a court appearance is mandatory.
    2. Do not ignore it. Missing deadlines can lead to licence suspension or a warrant.
    3. Weigh the true cost. Insurance increases over 3–5 years often exceed the fine itself.
    4. Request disclosure. Ask for evidence before trial; errors can lead to dismissal.
    5. For DUI, request the licence hearing immediately. Deadlines can be as short as 7–15 days.

    Documents to gather

    • Ticket / charge papers
    • Driver's licence and abstract
    • Insurance policy
    • Photos of the location and signage
    • Dash-cam footage

    What it may cost

    Traffic defence is commonly flat-fee. DUI defence costs more but the consequences (licence loss, record, insurance) justify professional help.

    When to talk to a lawyer

    Always for DUI, reckless driving, accidents with injury, commercial licences or when you are close to a suspension from points.

    Frequently asked questions

    Should I just pay the ticket?

    Paying is usually a conviction. Check the effect on points and insurance first.

    Can I refuse a breath test?

    Refusal often carries its own penalties and automatic suspension. Know your local law.

    Will a ticket from another state count?

    Often yes, through interstate compacts or reciprocal agreements.

    How long do points stay?

    Typically 2–3 years, but insurance companies may look back further.
