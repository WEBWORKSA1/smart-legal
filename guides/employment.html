    Employment & Workplace Rights

    Unpaid wages, wrongful dismissal, discrimination, harassment and leave — know what your employer can and cannot do.

    Employment law sets the minimum rules for the relationship between workers and employers: pay, hours, safety, leave and fair treatment. Many workers are 'at-will' or on contracts that can be ended, but no employer is allowed to break wage laws, discriminate or retaliate against someone for exercising their rights.

    Your key rights

    • To be paid at least the minimum wage for every hour worked, plus overtime where the law requires it.
    • To a workplace free of discrimination and harassment based on protected traits such as race, sex, religion, disability, age or pregnancy.
    • To protected leave in many situations (illness, family care, parental leave), depending on location and employer size.
    • Protection from retaliation for complaining about illegal conduct, unsafe conditions or unpaid wages.
    • In many places, to notice or pay in lieu of notice when dismissed without cause.
    • To your final pay on time after you leave.

    Step-by-step plan

    1. Write down what happened. Dates, times, who said what, witnesses. Contemporaneous notes are powerful evidence.
    2. Save documents you are entitled to. Your contract, offer letter, handbook, pay stubs, schedules and performance reviews.
    3. Use internal channels. A written complaint to HR creates a record and is often required before certain claims.
    4. Do not sign a severance agreement in a hurry. Severance packages usually require you to waive claims. You can ask for time to have it reviewed.
    5. Check agency deadlines. Discrimination complaints to government agencies can have very short filing windows — sometimes as little as 180 days.
    6. File with the right body. Wage claims, human-rights complaints and safety reports often go to different agencies.

    Documents to gather

    • Employment contract / offer letter
    • Pay stubs and timesheets
    • Employee handbook
    • Emails or messages about the issue
    • Performance reviews
    • Termination letter
    • Severance offer

    What it may cost

    Wage and discrimination agencies are usually free. Many employment lawyers offer contingency or hybrid fees for strong claims; some laws let a winning employee recover legal fees from the employer.

    When to talk to a lawyer

    Speak to a lawyer if you were fired soon after a complaint, injury, pregnancy or leave request; if you are offered severance; or if the amount of unpaid wages or overtime is significant.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I be fired for no reason?

    In at-will systems, often yes — but never for an illegal reason such as discrimination or retaliation. In many other countries, dismissal without cause requires notice or pay in lieu.

    Am I an employee or a contractor?

    It depends on control, integration and economic reality, not the label in your contract. Misclassification can cost you overtime and benefits.

    My employer has not paid me. What now?

    Send a written request, then file a wage claim with your labour department. Keep proof of hours worked.

    Is a non-compete enforceable?

    It varies widely. Some places ban most non-competes; others enforce them only if reasonable in time, area and scope.
