    Landlord & Tenant (Housing)

    Leases, repairs, deposits, rent increases and evictions — practical steps for renters and small landlords.

    Rental housing is one of the most regulated relationships in everyday law. Leases matter, but local landlord-tenant statutes often override anything in the lease that is less protective. Whether you rent or own, knowing the required notices and deadlines prevents most disputes.

    Your key rights

    • Tenants are generally entitled to a habitable home — working heat, water, plumbing, electricity and safe structure.
    • Landlords usually must give notice before entering, except in emergencies.
    • Security deposits must be handled by local rules, and many places require return or an itemised list of deductions within a fixed number of days.
    • Evictions require a legal process; changing locks, removing belongings or cutting utilities ('self-help eviction') is illegal in most places.
    • Retaliation against a tenant for requesting repairs or contacting housing inspectors is prohibited in many jurisdictions.
    • Landlords have the right to timely rent, reasonable care of the property and lawful use of the premises.

    Step-by-step plan

    1. Read the lease and local law together. The lease sets terms, but statutes can cap deposits, limit fees and require specific notices.
    2. Put requests in writing. Repair requests, notices and agreements should be dated and kept — email works.
    3. Document condition. Photograph or video the unit at move-in and move-out.
    4. Respond to eviction papers immediately. Court deadlines to answer can be only a few days. Not responding usually means losing by default.
    5. Use inspectors and mediation. Local code enforcement and tenant-landlord mediation services can resolve problems without court.
    6. Demand deposits properly. If a deposit is not returned on time, send a formal demand letter citing the deadline before filing in small claims.

    Documents to gather

    • Signed lease and renewals
    • Rent receipts / bank records
    • Move-in / move-out photos
    • Repair requests and replies
    • Notices received or sent
    • Inspection reports

    What it may cost

    Small-claims court filing fees are typically modest. Many cities fund free tenant legal services, especially for eviction defence.

    When to talk to a lawyer

    Get help immediately if you received eviction court papers, if you are being locked out or harassed, or if you are a landlord facing a tenant who refuses to leave after proper notice.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can my landlord raise the rent?

    Usually yes at renewal or with proper notice, but rent-controlled or rent-stabilised units have caps. Use our rent-increase checker to see the percentage.

    Can I withhold rent for repairs?

    Only in places that allow it and only after following strict steps. Doing it wrong can lead to eviction.

    How fast can I be evicted?

    Timelines vary from a few weeks to several months depending on notice periods and court schedules.

    What can be deducted from my deposit?

    Typically unpaid rent and damage beyond normal wear and tear — not routine repainting or ordinary carpet wear.
