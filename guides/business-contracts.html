    Small Business & Contracts

    Choosing a structure, writing contracts that hold up, getting paid and resolving disputes without wrecking relationships.

    Most small-business legal problems trace back to a missing or vague agreement. A clear written contract — who does what, by when, for how much, and what happens if it goes wrong — prevents more disputes than any lawsuit can fix. This guide covers the essentials every founder, freelancer and small company should have in place.

    Your key rights

    • A contract is generally enforceable if there is an offer, acceptance, something of value exchanged and an intent to be bound.
    • Many contracts can be oral, but some (like real-estate sales or long-term agreements) usually must be in writing.
    • Limited companies and LLCs can protect owners' personal assets from business debts if formalities are respected.
    • You can usually recover money owed through demand letters, small-claims court or collections.
    • Electronic signatures are legally valid for most contracts in most countries.

    Step-by-step plan

    1. Choose and register the right structure. Sole proprietor, partnership, LLC/limited company or corporation — consider liability, tax and investors.
    2. Use written agreements for every relationship. Clients, contractors, co-founders, suppliers. Our NDA and service-agreement templates are a starting point.
    3. Define scope, price, timeline and termination. Ambiguity in these four items causes most disputes.
    4. Protect your name and ideas. Check trademark availability before branding; use confidentiality agreements before sharing secrets.
    5. Keep separate finances. Mixing personal and business money can weaken liability protection.
    6. Escalate disputes in steps. Conversation → written notice → demand letter → mediation → small claims or court.

    Documents to gather

    • Formation documents and registrations
    • Operating / shareholder agreement
    • Client and contractor contracts
    • Invoices and payment records
    • Licences and permits
    • Insurance policies

    What it may cost

    Registration fees are usually modest. Many lawyers offer fixed-fee packages for formation or contract review. Small claims is the cheapest route for unpaid invoices under the local limit.

    When to talk to a lawyer

    Use a lawyer for co-founder or investor agreements, large contracts, regulated industries, employees, real-estate leases and any dispute above the small-claims limit.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is an email agreement binding?

    It can be, if it shows offer, acceptance and agreed terms. A signed document is far easier to prove.

    Do I need an LLC?

    Not always, but it can separate personal and business liability. Tax treatment differs by country.

    A client will not pay. What should I do?

    Send a formal demand letter with a deadline, then consider small claims. Our generator and interest calculator help.

    What should every contract include?

    Parties, scope, price and payment terms, deadlines, IP ownership, confidentiality, termination, dispute resolution and governing law.
