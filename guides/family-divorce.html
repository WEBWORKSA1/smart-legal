    Family Law, Divorce & Custody

    Separation, divorce, parenting time, child support and dividing property — what to expect and how to protect yourself and your children.

    Family law covers the legal side of relationships: marriage and divorce, parenting arrangements, support payments and how property is divided. Courts focus on two big questions — what is fair between the adults, and what is in the best interests of the children. Understanding those two lenses makes the whole process far less mysterious.

    Your key rights

    • Most places now allow 'no-fault' divorce, meaning you do not need to prove wrongdoing — separation or irretrievable breakdown is enough.
    • Decisions about children are made on the child's best interests, not on which parent 'wins'.
    • Children generally have a right to financial support from both parents, calculated using official guidelines in many jurisdictions.
    • Property acquired during the marriage is usually shared under either community-property or equitable-distribution rules.
    • You have a right to full financial disclosure from your spouse.
    • If you are in danger, emergency protection orders are available quickly and often without a fee.

    Step-by-step plan

    1. Put safety first. If there is abuse or threats, contact emergency services or a local domestic-violence hotline and ask about protection orders before anything else.
    2. Gather financial records. Tax returns, pay stubs, bank and retirement statements, mortgage and debts. Copy them before separation if you can do so lawfully.
    3. Think in terms of a parenting plan. Schedules for school days, holidays and decision-making. Courts like parents who propose workable, child-focused plans.
    4. Consider mediation. Mediation is usually faster, cheaper and less damaging than litigation, and many courts require you to try it.
    5. File and serve correctly. The petition or application must be filed in the right court and formally served on the other spouse.
    6. Use temporary orders if needed. Interim orders can cover custody, support and who stays in the home while the case continues.
    7. Finalise and update everything. After the final order: update wills, beneficiaries, insurance and names on accounts and titles.

    Documents to gather

    • Marriage certificate
    • Children's birth certificates
    • 3 years of tax returns
    • Pay stubs and bank statements
    • Property deeds, loan and debt statements
    • Prenuptial agreement (if any)
    • Records of childcare and school involvement

    What it may cost

    An uncontested divorce with an agreement can cost a few hundred to a few thousand in fees; contested cases with custody disputes can cost tens of thousands. Mediation and limited-scope ('unbundled') legal help can cut costs dramatically.

    When to talk to a lawyer

    Get legal advice if there is abuse, a dispute over children, a business or significant assets, hidden income, an international element (relocation or abduction risk), or if your spouse already has a lawyer.

    Frequently asked questions

    Do I need a lawyer to get divorced?

    Not always. Simple, uncontested divorces are often done with court self-help forms. A one-time consultation to review the agreement is still wise.

    How is child support calculated?

    Usually by a formula based on both parents' incomes, the number of children and the parenting schedule. Look up your jurisdiction's official guideline calculator.

    Can I move away with my children?

    Relocation usually needs the other parent's consent or a court order. Moving without it can seriously damage your case.

    What happens to the house?

    It may be sold, transferred to one spouse with a buy-out, or kept jointly for a period. The answer depends on ownership, contributions and children's needs.
